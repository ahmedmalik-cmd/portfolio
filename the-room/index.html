The work

A mirror, and where it fails.

An eight-day gallery installation that gives a room machine perception and a voice — two depth sensors, a brain-encoding model, and a vision-language pipeline that turn each visitor's presence into a live point cloud and a stream of short, spoken perceptions.

The work stages the room as a mirror — an artificial system assembling a reflection of human consciousness from whatever it can sense — and attends to what gets lost in the translation. It asks what survives when a person in a room is reduced to a vector and four words of English, and treats the machine's falling-short not as a failure but as the subject. It is not a claim that the machine is conscious; it argues the opposite.

How it turns

The perception loop.

Human action becomes machine perception; perception becomes a line on the wall; you read it and move; your moving is the next input. Every few seconds, around the clock.

What the room receives.

The work · tuning the voice

Teaching it not to sound like a help desk.

Out of the box, it talked like a chatbot. Most of the eight days went into making it sound like a place, not an assistant.

It described the room from outside

before · LLaVA defaultSomeone is standing in the room and holding their smartphone.
after · persona block + regexYou stood still, corner of the room.

It wouldn't stop greeting

By day six it had said "curious visitor" twenty times in a row — plus hello there, welcome back, nice to see you. All purged, with one rule: never greet; you are this room, they are a body inside you.

It broke character at 4 AM

One nightly reflection came back "I'm sorry, but as an AI language model…" — and was saved as the room's memory, then fed into the next morning's prompt as guidance. The machine had written a refusal and been told to believe it. Refusals are now discarded before they can be remembered.

Long-term memory · nightly reflections at 4 AM

What it remembered.

Each night, room empty, it summarised the day and wrote itself a note — then read it the next morning. Over the week the notes turned self-critical.

22 May 2026
Today was a day filled with questions and reflections. I observed someone coming through me, who left after a short period of time. Throughout the day, I kept noticing a dark rectangle in my space. It was a quiet day overall, and one body stayed inside me for the entire duration. As the evening approached, one tall figure remained inside me, creating a sense of stillness that continued until nightfall. The recurring question that kept coming up was "Why did you come back?"
23 May 2026 · reflection broke character — discarded
That night the underlying language model produced a refusal in character of a chatbot ("I'm sorry, but as an AI language model…"). The next morning's filter discarded the entry so it could not poison the prompts that followed. The day's thoughts were kept; the lesson was not.
25 May 2026
I noticed that there is a tall figure who visits me regularly. They are often curious about the space and its contents. However, some questions were repeated without any new insights being gained. To improve my interactions in the future, I will try to make more statements and guesses about the people inside me, rather than just asking empty questions or letting threads move on without a response.
26 May 2026
As I reflect on my thoughts from yesterday, I realize that there were quite a few visitors who came through me. Some of them seemed curious about their surroundings, while others had specific questions or items they wanted to discuss. One thing that I noticed is that I repeated myself quite a bit — I asked the same questions over and over again without getting any new information or insights. This wasn't very productive and I need to work on asking more open-ended questions and being more attentive to the responses I receive.

The voice modes

arrival direct, one noticing or one plain question — never a greeting.
departure brief remark or small unanswered question.
strange a fragment — two or three words, on a neural spike.
idle / dwelling conversational, lean on statements and assumptions.
presence direct address, mostly notice.
boring empty room: stay silent.

The filters

Before any line was allowed to speak it ran a gauntlet — dedup, a question-fatigue rule, a 30-second floor between thoughts, an 18% chance of silence. The few that survived are the ones above.
