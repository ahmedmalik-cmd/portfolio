03Engineering notes

What broke, and the fix

From the real & rendered build log — problems that surfaced live, in front of visitors.

982Thoughts spoken
45Visits recorded
13h 18mOccupied
8Days live

Latency

12 s → 5 s per thought

Capture, brain model, language model and caption ran in series — two thoughts for a 30-second visit. A single-slot clip producer and an async brain worker decoupled them; the language model now reads the freshest result instead of waiting.

Signal

A brain signal that never moved

Raw activations sat between 0.00 and 0.01, so nothing downstream changed. Each value is rescaled against its own rolling 10th–90th percentile (300 samples) — real range, plus a trigger for a “strange mode”.

Resilience

Keep thinking when the GPU host drops

One failed parse used to crash the loop. In degraded mode the client logs once, feeds synthetic zeros so the local model keeps speaking, and re-probes /health every 60 s.

Recall

Telling two visitors apart

Colour histograms could tell day from night, not one person from another. A CLIP ViT-B-32 server embeds every arrival (512-d); cosine similarity, tuned to 0.84, lets the room say “you came back” and mean it.

real & rendered

Senior thesis · Bennington College
Usdan Gallery · 18–26 May 2026

An eight-day installation that thinks about the people inside it. Two depth sensors build a live point cloud of the gallery; a local vision-language model — fed by a brain-encoding model, pose tracking and visual memory — says what it notices on the third wall. Nothing leaves the building.

982Thoughts spoken
45Visits recorded
13h 18mOccupied
8Days live

Spec sheet

Sensors
2 × Kinect v1 · depth + RGB, 640 × 480 @ 30 fps · opposing corners, ~3 m apart
Point cloud
Unity · custom compute + shader · ICP-aligned · temporal smoothing · persistent background plate
Perception loop
Python · four parallel workers · 2 s clips @ 8 fps
Models
LLaVA via Ollama (local) · TRIBE v2 brain encoder · open_clip ViT-B-32 · MediaPipe Face Mesh + Pose
Compute
Installation PC + RTX 3090 on the LAN · REST: /predict, /embed
Display
3 projectors — 2 × point cloud, 1 × captions · in-piece calibration
Memory
JSON on disk — every thought, visit and nightly reflection
Input
Moog Theremini · MIDI → OSC → cloud hue
Network
Fully local · degraded mode if the GPU host drops

What I built

The system, end to end: a Unity bridge that fuses two Kinect v1 depth streams into one live point cloud, with presence and per-person height tracking; a Python perception loop that runs its workers in parallel so nothing blocks; a GPU machine on the LAN serving a TRIBE v2 brain-encoding model and a CLIP embedding server; and the prompt assembly that turns seven streams of context into one line from a local LLaVA model — projected on the third wall, written to memory, and reflected on every night at 4 AM.

How it works

Two depth sensors in opposite corners stream to the Unity bridge, which merges them into one cloud for two projectors. The RGB stream feeds the perception loop: 2-second clips go to the brain-encoding model, single frames to pose tracking and CLIP recall. Their outputs, the time, the visitor’s history and last night’s note are assembled into a prompt for the local vision-language model. Its line goes to the third projector, and into memory.

Engineering notes

Latency

12 s → 5 s per thought

The pipeline ran in series. A single-slot clip producer and an async brain worker decoupled it; the language model reads the freshest result instead of blocking.

Signal

Adaptive normalisation

Brain activations were flat (0.00–0.01). Rescaling against a rolling 10th–90th percentile over 300 samples gave the signal range — and a “strange mode” trigger.

Resilience

Degraded mode

If the GPU host fails, the client feeds synthetic zeros so the local model keeps speaking, and re-probes /health every 60 s.

Recall

CLIP over colour histograms

512-d ViT-B-32 embeddings per arrival; cosine similarity tuned to 0.84 so returning visitors are recognised by body, not lighting.

Voice

Not a help desk

A first-person persona, regex filters for third-person and greeter phrasing, a fixation analyser, and a 30-second gate between thoughts during long visits.

Rendering

Captions eaten by the cloud

The caption canvas was depth-tested against the point cloud. Moved to a screen-space overlay, with a backing band and outlined glyphs.

What it said

  • You stood still, corner of the room.
  • Your left hand is resting on the floor while your right hand is slightly raised.
  • You came back.
  • You, right here, now.

Projection mapping

Jadu × AWE — Augmented World Expo
Mixer · volunteer projection & AV

Volunteering with Jadu, an AR company, I ran the projection for their mixer at Augmented World Expo end to end — rigging, mapping and running a 20,000-lumen laser projector, then striking and packing it.

Spec sheet

Projector
Panasonic PT-MZ20K
Light
20,000 lm · 3LCD laser · liquid-cooled
Native
WUXGA 1920 × 1200, driven 1:1
Content
Unity
Output
Off-target areas masked to true black
Rig
~20 kg body, two-person lift · lens transported separately
Role
Setup → mapping → live operation → strike

What I did

I rigged and configured the projector, drove the mapped content from Unity and aligned it to the venue on site. Output ran at the projector’s native 1920 × 1200, with everything off the target masked to true black — at 20,000 lumens, spill is real. I operated it live through the event, then struck the rig.

Handling. The PT-MZ20K is sealed and liquid-cooled, so breakdown means the lens and the rig, not the chassis: tool-free lens removal — top slide cover, lock lever, counter-clockwise out — the lens cased on its own, the body cased level and left to reach room temperature before power-up, to avoid condensation.
