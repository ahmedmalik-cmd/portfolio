real & rendered

— who decides which is what?

Senior thesis · Bennington College
Usdan Gallery · 18–26 May 2026

The closer I got to systems that could read and respond to human presence, the more I became interested in what they were missing.

The system view — sensors to wall in seven components. The concept and the voice live on the project page.

Spec sheet

Runtime
8 days, continuous, in public
Sensors
2 × Kinect v1 · depth + RGB, 640 × 480 @ 30 fps
Point cloud
~140,000 points per frame · Unity · custom compute + shader
Compute
Installation PC + RTX 3090 on the LAN · REST: /predict, /embed · fully local
Models
LLaVA via Ollama (local) · TRIBE v2 · open_clip ViT-B-32 · MediaPipe Face Mesh + Pose
Latency
~5 s per thought, down from ~12 s
Display
3 projectors — 2 × point cloud, 1 × captions

System map

Select a component — what it does, where it runs, what broke and the fix.

Sense

2 × Kinect v1

Hardware · opposing corners, ~3 m apart

Does

Depth + RGB at 640 × 480, 30 fps each, angled inward and slightly down so the two views overlap across the room.

What broke · the fix

The sensor frames are aligned by ICP. A “place sensors from blueprint” shortcut snapped both back to rough positions mid-run and tore a seam through the cloud — now flagged as destructive; the saved calibration is reloaded instead.

  • Kinect v1
  • Depth + RGB
  • ICP calibration
Fuse

Unity bridge

Installation PC · Unity / C#

Does

Merges both clouds, smooths depth over time, fills brief dropouts from a persistent background plate, detects presence and per-person heights, and writes presence.json for the perception loop.

What broke · the fix

The plate kept restoring depth captured at an old 4 m range after the range was raised to 8 m, slicing the far wall off. It now resets itself whenever min or max depth changes.

  • Unity
  • C#
  • Compute shader
  • BridgePointCloudManager.cs
Perceive

Perception loop

Installation PC · Python

Does

Pulls the merged RGB stream and runs workers in parallel — 2-second clips at 8 fps for the brain model, single frames for MediaPipe pose (2 Hz) and recall — then assembles seven streams of context into one prompt.

What broke · the fix

It started serial — capture, brain, language model, caption — about 12 s per thought. A single-slot clip producer and an async brain worker let the language model read the freshest result instead of waiting: about 5 s.

  • Python
  • Threading
  • MediaPipe
  • tribe_kinect_client.py
GPU host

TRIBE v2 + CLIP

RTX 3090 on the LAN · REST

Does

TRIBE v2 predicts cortical activation from each 2-second clip (POST /predict). open_clip ViT-B-32 embeds every arrival as a 512-d vector (POST /embed), so the room can recognize a return.

What broke · the fix

Raw activations sat between 0.00 and 0.01, so they’re rescaled against a rolling 10th–90th percentile (300 samples). If the host drops, the client feeds synthetic zeros and re-probes /health every 60 s. Recall matches at cosine 0.84.

  • TRIBE v2
  • open_clip
  • REST
  • RTX 3090
Speak

LLaVA via Ollama

Installation PC · local, offline

Does

Turns the assembled prompt and the current frame into one short line — a noticing, a guess, a question — every thirty seconds or so.

What broke · the fix

It defaulted to help-desk speech. Shaped against the logs: a first-person persona, regex filters for third-person and greeter phrasing, a fixation analyzer that steers off repeated words, and a 30-second gate during long visits.

  • Ollama
  • LLaVA
  • Prompt assembly
  • Output filters
Show

3 projectors

Daisy-chained extended displays

Does

Two project the merged point cloud, calibrated in-piece; the third shows the room’s line on black, over a top-down view of the cloud.

What broke · the fix

The caption canvas was depth-tested against the cloud, so points drew over the text. It moved to a screen-space overlay with a backing band and outlined glyphs, under one CanvasGroup so the fades still animate together.

  • Unity
  • Multi-display
  • CaptionProjector.cs
Memory

JSON on disk

room_memory.json · visits.json · Episodes/

Does

Keeps every thought and every visit — arrival, duration, CLIP signature, thumbnail — plus a reflection written at 4 AM with the room empty, which is injected into the next day’s prompt.

What broke · the fix

One night the reflection came back as a chatbot refusal and was stored as memory. Replies with refusal patterns are now discarded before saving, and filtered again at injection.

  • JSON
  • Nightly reflection
  • Memory injection

Why it’s built this way

Measure, then guessWhat we can understand, we can simulate.

It measures everything it can — depth, pose, brain response, a visual signature — and still only guesses. The line it speaks is a noticing, not a reading.

Its own measureMachines can’t be held to our metrics.

The brain signal only meant something once it was scored against its own history — a rolling 10th–90th percentile, not a human scale.

Who decidesReal and rendered — who decides which is what?

The rendered room on two walls, its reading of you on the third. The visitor stands between them.

Projection mapping

Jadu × AWE — Augmented World Expo
Mixer · volunteer projection & AV

Volunteering with Jadu, an AR company, I ran the projection for their mixer at Augmented World Expo on my own, end to end — transporting, assembling and rigging a 20,000-lumen laser projector, mapping and running it live, then disassembling and packing it.

Spec sheet

Projector
Panasonic PT-MZ20K
Light
20,000 lm · 3LCD laser · liquid-cooled
Native
WUXGA 1920 × 1200, driven 1:1
Content
Unity · TouchDesigner
Output
Off-target areas masked to true black
Rig
~20 kg body · lens transported separately
Role
Solo · transport → setup → mapping → live operation → strike

What I did

I rigged and configured the projector, drove the mapped content from Unity and TouchDesigner, and aligned it to the venue on site. Output ran at the projector’s native 1920 × 1200, with everything off the target masked to true black — at 20,000 lumens, spill is real. I operated it live through the event, then struck the rig.

Handling. All of the physical work was mine alone — transport, assembly, rigging, disassembly and packing. The PT-MZ20K is sealed and liquid-cooled, so breakdown means the lens and the rig, not the chassis: tool-free lens removal (top slide cover, lock lever, counter-clockwise out), the lens cased on its own, and the body cased level and left to reach room temperature before power-up, to avoid condensation.
